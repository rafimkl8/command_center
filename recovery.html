The one rule that changes everything

AI may explain, review, and quiz me. AI may not author. The moment AI writes it, your brain marks the problem "solved" and files nothing. That is exactly how you arrived at Week 19 feeling empty.
25 minutes cold, then help. Every problem gets a genuine solo attempt on a timer before anything else opens.
Blank page before every topic. Write what you remember from memory first. Then study. Then rewrite. The gap between the two is the only thing that actually stuck.
Reconstruct from empty. Every topic ends with you building one small thing — 20 to 40 lines — from an empty file, no reference, no tab open.
Revisit at +2 days and +7 days. Ten minutes, blank page. This is what turns a week of study into knowledge you still own in November.
If you do use AI to ship something, log it as a 1 in the audit in the same sitting. Not later, not next week — before you close the laptop. What damaged Weeks 8–18 was not using AI. It was using AI and not writing it down, so the gap went silent and compounded for three months. A tracked debt is survivable. An invisible one is what put you here.

The two holes are one hole

The audit says Weeks 3–4 are a gap and all of Django is a gap. Those are not two problems. Django is not a new language stacked on Python — it is Python's class system with a database bolted on. Everything you flagged in Week 4 is something Django uses on every single page.

So do not study Python and then study Django. Learn the Python in Django's vocabulary and every hour counts twice:

Each day: blank page from memory → learn → rebuild from empty. Never the other order.

Now build the same app again, in Django. Same domain, so every ounce of attention goes to what Django adds rather than to the problem itself.

And look at what Week 19 actually is: "Django To-Do App Project." Your backfill deliverable and your current assignment are the same artifact. You are not choosing between catching up and keeping up.

Weeks 20–24: the part I will not pretend about

Six consecutive Django weeks are in front of you and you are starting from zero. You will not do the Week 20 blog and the Week 21 e-commerce project at full depth. Decide that now, deliberately, instead of discovering it in a panic at 2am.

Week 21 — 48 hours, 6 live classes. Attend every class; the exposure is worth real money even when you follow only half. Take notes. Then build the smallest honest slice yourself: product list, product detail, add to cart. Nothing else.

An ugly three-page shop you can explain line by line beats a complete storefront you cannot. The first one is a portfolio piece and an interview story. The second is a liability that will be exposed in twenty minutes by anyone competent.

Daily shape — and the part that is not negotiable

Before the break (Aug 5–14): about 4 focused hours a day. During the break: 6 focused hours, 6 days a week, one full day off. Not 12 hours. You are rebuilding from a fragile place, and a plan that burns you out in week one is worse than no plan at all.

  • Morning block (post-Fajr, ~3h) — hardest new concept of the day. This is your best brain; do not spend it on video.
  • Afternoon block (~2h) — reconstruct from empty. Build the small thing. No tutorials.
  • Evening (~1h) — spaced review of +2 day and +7 day items, then write the day's one-paragraph explanation.
  • Evenings after Isha belong to your wife. Non-negotiable, not a reward for finishing.

Why this makes you an engineer, not a coder

Writing code from a clear spec is the part AI genuinely took. That was the old entry-level job and it is not coming back. What went up in value is everything around it: deciding what to build, decomposing a vague problem, spotting the subtly wrong answer, knowing what breaks at 10x load.

You cannot review what you cannot read. That is the whole argument. The person who can only prompt is the person who gets replaced; the person who can verify is the one who gets paid. Your instinct to learn this properly is not idealism — it is the employability strategy.

So from now on, every project gets three sentences written in its README:

  • Why did I choose this design over the obvious alternative?
  • What breaks first if traffic goes up 10x?
  • What would I do differently with another week?

Three sentences. No extra study hours. That is the judgment muscle that separates the two job titles, and it is also the exact material interviews are made of.
