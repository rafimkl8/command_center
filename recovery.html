Order is dependency order, not calendar order. Clear a track before starting the next one — otherwise you build on sand again.

The one rule that changes everything

AI may explain, review, and quiz me. AI may not author. The moment AI writes it, your brain marks the problem "solved" and files nothing. That is exactly how you arrived at Week 19 feeling empty.
25 minutes cold, then help. Every problem gets a genuine solo attempt on a timer before anything else opens.
Blank page before every topic. Write what you remember from memory first. Then study. Then rewrite. The gap between the two is the only thing that actually stuck.
Reconstruct from empty. Every topic ends with you building one small thing — 20 to 40 lines — from an empty file, no reference, no tab open.
Revisit at +2 days and +7 days. Ten minutes, blank page. This is what turns a week of study into knowledge you still own in November.

Term break sprint — Aug 15 to Sep 2

Nineteen days with no university load. This window is roughly the size of your entire conceptual debt. It will not come again before the job hunt starts.

Daily shape during the break: 6 focused hours, 6 days a week, one full day off. Not 12 hours. You are rebuilding from a fragile place, and a plan that burns you out in week one is worse than no plan.

  • Morning block (post-Fajr, ~3h) — hardest new concept of the day. This is your best brain; do not spend it on video.
  • Afternoon block (~2h) — reconstruct from empty. Build the small thing. No tutorials.
  • Evening (~1h) — spaced review of +2 day and +7 day items, then write the day's one-paragraph explanation.
  • Evenings after Isha belong to your wife. Non-negotiable, not a reward for finishing.

Why this makes you an engineer, not a coder

Writing code from a clear spec is the part AI genuinely took. That was the old entry-level job and it is not coming back. What went up in value is everything around it: deciding what to build, decomposing a vague problem, spotting the subtly wrong answer, knowing what breaks at 10x load.

You cannot review what you cannot read. That is the whole argument. The person who can only prompt is the person who gets replaced; the person who can verify is the one who gets paid. Your instinct to learn this properly is not idealism — it is the employability strategy.

So from now on, every project gets three sentences written in its README:

  • Why did I choose this design over the obvious alternative?
  • What breaks first if traffic goes up 10x?
  • What would I do differently with another week?

Three sentences. No extra study hours. That is the judgment muscle that separates the two job titles, and it is also the exact material interviews are made of.
