💼 The freelance journey (start ~Module 22)

From Bangladesh, freelancing is usually your fastest first income and fastest real-world experience — lower barrier than a salaried remote role, and every gig gives you reviews, references, and portfolio proof. Goal: first paid dollar by end of 2026. Don't start before ~Module 22 though — protect your learning first.

Mindset shift: freelancing is a business, not "doing tasks for money." You're selling an outcome to a client who has a problem. The dev who communicates clearly and hits deadlines beats the better coder who goes silent — every time.
Phase F1 · Module 20–22 · Build the storefront

🎯 Pick a niche (don't be "a web developer")

  • Niche = easier to get hired and charge more. Be "Django + React developer for small businesses" or "AI-integration developer (chatbots/RAG for startups)" — your differentiator.
  • Generalists drown in competition; specialists get chosen.

🧩 Set up your platforms

Upwork (primary)Fiverr (productized gigs)Freelancer.comPeoplePerHourContraLocal: BDjobs, FB groups, agencies
  • Upwork — best for hourly/contract work via proposals. Your main engine.
  • Fiverr — productized "gigs" clients buy directly (e.g., "I will build a Django REST API"). Set up 2–3.
  • Toptal / Arc / Lemon.io — vetted, higher pay. Come back to these once you're stronger (2027).
💳 Getting paid from Bangladesh: the standard route is Payoneer — connect it to Upwork/Fiverr/Freelancer, receive USD, then withdraw to your local bank in BDT. (Wise has receiving limits in BD; Payoneer is the reliable default.) Keep clean records — Bangladesh offers remittance incentives for freelance earnings. Set this up before your first gig so payment is frictionless.

✅ F1 setup checklist

Phase F2 · Module 22–26 · Land the first 3 reviews

The first 5 reviews are the hardest and the most valuable thing you own. Bid on small, crystal-clear jobs you can absolutely nail.

✍️ How to write proposals that win

  • Never copy-paste. First line addresses their specific problem — proves you read the post.
  • Give a tiny plan (3 bullet steps) showing you already understand it.
  • Link one relevant sample (not your whole portfolio).
  • Keep it short. End with a question/CTA ("Want me to start with X?").
  • Apply 5–10 quality proposals/day early on. It's a numbers game at first.
🚩 Scam / red flags — walk away: asking to pay or talk outside the platform, "do a free test project," vague scope with big promises, pressure to start before any agreement, or rates that seem too good to be true.

🤝 Deliver like a pro (this earns the 5 stars)

  • Confirm scope in writing before starting. Break into milestones.
  • Over-communicate: a short update every 1–2 days. Reply within 24h.
  • Under-promise, over-deliver. Hit every deadline.
  • At the end, politely ask for a review + ask if they need anything else.

✅ F2 first-client checklist

Phase F3 · Module 26–32 · Momentum → higher rates
  • After 3–5 five-star reviews, raise your rates — your proof now sells for you.
  • Ask happy clients for repeat work, referrals, and a LinkedIn testimonial.
  • Lean your gigs toward AI integration — fewer competitors, premium pricing.
  • Track income, hours, and clients in a simple sheet.
Phase F4 · 2027 · Scale & go premium
  • Apply to vetted networks (Toptal, Arc, Lemon.io) for higher-paying remote work.
  • Move toward retainer clients (steady monthly income) and productized offers.
  • Raise rates regularly. Learn to say no to bad-fit clients — your time is the asset now.
Why this matters beyond money: freelance experience is real experience. "Delivered 8 projects for international clients with 5-star ratings" on your CV beats "completed a bootcamp" in any salaried-job interview.

🎯 Job hunt — three lanes, run in parallel

Don't bet on one path. Run all three at once and take whichever offer comes first. For a beginner in Bangladesh, the realistic order of "easiest first win" is usually: local + freelance → remote → big-tech remote.

📈 Application strategy (this is where people lose)

  • Referrals first — they roughly double your callback rate. Before applying cold, check if you know anyone there (LinkedIn) and ask for a referral or a 15-min chat.
  • Volume + tailoring: aim for 10 applications/week. Tailor your CV's top line + the first 2 sentences of each cover letter to the role.
  • Apply at ~60% match. Job posts are wish-lists. If you meet most of it, apply — don't wait to feel "ready."
  • Track everything in a sheet: company · role · date · status · follow-up date. Treat the hunt like engineering.
  • Follow up politely after ~1 week of silence.
  • Apply early — being in the first 25 applicants matters a lot.

✅ Job-hunt setup checklist

📄 CV / resume essentials

  • One page. Simple, ATS-friendly format (no fancy columns that parsers choke on).
  • Top: Name · title ("Full-Stack Developer | Python · Django · React · AI") · email · GitHub · portfolio · LinkedIn · location.
  • Projects FIRST (you're junior — projects are your proof): 3 best, each with what it does, the tech, and a live link. Add impact where possible.
  • Experience: freelance counts! "Freelance Full-Stack Developer — delivered X projects, 5-star rated."
  • Skills + Education: UoPeople BS Computer Science (in progress).
  • Mirror keywords from the job post (ATS scans for them). Use action verbs. Cut all fluff.
Your self-taught edge: a green GitHub graph, deployed projects with live links, and an AI flagship beat a generic degree-only candidate. Make your proof impossible to miss.

🧠 Interview mastery

Interviewing is a separate skill from coding — and it's learnable. Practice it deliberately (mock interviews on Pramp / interviewing.io, or with peers). Most candidates lose not on ability but on preparation and communication.

StageWhat it tests
1. Recruiter screenFit, motivation, communication, salary expectations, logistics
2. Coding / technicalDSA (LeetCode-style) or a practical build — and how you think
3. System design (light for juniors)Vocabulary + trade-offs (load balancer, cache, DB, scaling)
4. BehavioralTeamwork, conflict, ownership, culture fit (STAR)
5. Final / founderVision fit, your questions, the human read

💻 Coding interview — do & don't

✅ Do

  • Clarify before coding — restate the problem, ask about inputs, edge cases, constraints.
  • Think out loud — they're hiring your reasoning, not just the final answer.
  • Start with a brute-force idea, then optimize. State time/space complexity.
  • Test your code with an example and an edge case before saying "done."
  • If stuck, say what you're thinking — it becomes a collaboration, and hints flow.

🚫 Don't

  • Code in silence · jump straight to code without clarifying · pretend to know something · give up quietly · ignore edge cases.
Your benchmark (from the plan): any LeetCode easy in <10 min, medium in <25 min, narrating your thinking. Hit that and you'll clear most junior screens.

🗣️ Behavioral — the STAR method

Answer "tell me about a time…" with Situation → Task → Action → Result. Quantify the result. Prepare 6–8 stories in advance so you're never caught flat:

  • A hard bug you solved · a tight deadline you met · learning something fast
  • A disagreement / handling feedback · a project you're proud of
  • A failure + what you learned · juggling competing priorities (you have a great one: degree + full-stack + family!)

❓ Questions YOU must ask them (never say "no questions")

  • What does success look like in the first 90 days?
  • How does the team review code / mentor juniors?
  • What's the biggest challenge the team faces right now?
  • How does the team use AI in its workflow today?
  • What's the tech stack and how are decisions made?

✅ Interview-prep checklist

📦 Take-home projects

  • Scope it — don't over-engineer, but show quality: a clean README, a few tests, tidy commits.
  • Deliver on time. Add one thoughtful extra that shows initiative.
  • Document your decisions ("I chose X because…") — that's what they actually grade.

💰 Salary negotiation (yes, even as a junior)

  • Research ranges first: levels.fyi, Glassdoor, local norms. Know the number before they ask.
  • If pushed for a number first, give a researched range, not a single figure.
  • The first offer is rarely final. Politely ask "is there flexibility?" — a calm ask often gets 10–20% more.
  • Early career, weigh learning + mentorship + growth heavily, not just salary.
  • For remote-from-BD: know your worth, but price to win the role and raise later via proof.

📨 Before & after every interview

  • Before: research the company + interviewer, prep stories, quiet space, good light, water, GitHub/portfolio open, join 2 min early.
  • After: send a short thank-you note within 24h; reflect on what to improve; follow up after a week of silence.
Common killers to avoid: bad-mouthing anyone · lying about skills · zero enthusiasm · rambling · not knowing your own resume/projects · a messy remote setup. Any one of these can end it.
